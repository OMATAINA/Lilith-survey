Introduction
Omataina
Our publishing house

OMATAINA. Formed from the Quenya noun oma, meaning "word" or "speech", and the adjective taina, meaning "lengthened" or "extended", the name together evokes an assembly or community as places where the word is "extended" and shared, but also the book itself as a mechanism for "extending" the "word" across space and time. This is the name we have chosen for our publishing house: a group of friends with different skills, enthusiasts of literature and the visual arts, who have come together to begin a shared editorial project.

J. R. R. Tolkien has been a fundamental influence on the development of our literary and aesthetic sensibility, and so this publishing house is founded as a celebration of that unavoidable fantastic heritage. We are a community of the word, and like grandpa Tolkien, we want the protagonists of this adventure to be the ordinary folk, those debut artists who step out for the first time from the comfort of their shire and set off into this harsh world of commercial struggle. This community was founded to accompany our halflings on that journey.

But, just as Frodo and Bilbo did when they set out on the road, we want to know whether we have everything we need for our adventure to succeed. That is why we are asking you for about five minutes of your time to answer a survey about the first editorial project of our fantasy imprint, The Hidden Tower.

Lilith
George MacDonald · first editorial project

We want to begin our editorial adventure by publishing one of the most important works of fantasy by George MacDonald: Lilith. Without giving too much away, it is the story of Lilith's redemption. Regarded in certain versions of Genesis as the first wife of Adam, she is undoubtedly a character who has been invested in many tales and interpretations with the attributes of Milton's Lucifer: rebellion, nonconformism and a desire for creative power. She has been read in many ways, both admiringly and critically, but her prominence within Christian mythology cannot be denied.

MacDonald's work is narrated from the perspective of an ordinary man, Vane, who accidentally enters Lilith's world through a mirror. Across a series of chapters we will accompany him on his path of self-discovery and of coming to understand that world. All of this takes place in a story in which George MacDonald shows us, physically and tangibly, the corrupting power of pride over the world (with its barren, jagged, twisted landscapes full of irregular creatures and merciless characters), but also the transforming power of virtue.

This is the story with which we want to begin our editorial adventure at Omataina. This will not be a standard edition. We are interested in showing MacDonald's imaginings visually, and so we want to publish a graphic novel adaptation in which the world George MacDonald envisioned can be appreciated in images. Art and visual style
What styles would you like to see in this graphic novel?
When planning this edition, the editorial team put together a moodboard of works whose styles (level of detail in characters and backgrounds, colour palettes, luminosity, shadows, line types, degree of realism, etc.) we believe best suit the work. We would like to know your opinion. Without giving anything away, we believe there are four types of setting throughout the novel, each of which should be illustrated in a distinct style: one everyday setting (Victorian Gothic: mansions, Oxford, England), one macabre or what Todorov called the "uncanny" (unusual creatures, dark forests, barren landscapes, spectres, blood, rot, corruption), one bucolic and faerie (similar to what we associate with the fantasy of the hobbit world), and one cosmically transcendental (spatial, mystical, timeless, yet archaic).
